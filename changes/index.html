Changes

What has been added to this history and what has been corrected, newest first. Every addition and revision to the family tree, its sources and its open questions is recorded here as the research proceeds.

12 November 2026

  • New photographs (family album, 1948–1992). Published 32 scanned family photographs from the shared Google Photos album (Danenberg family Google Photos album, 33 scans in all), covering the family at Christmas in the DP camp in Germany around 1948, in the United States from the 1950s to the 1990s, at Liga Irene Danenberg's 1991 graduation, and on a c. 1991 family trip to Latvia. An initial automated pass over the album had viewed each photograph once at reduced size and written out each person from memory—describing about eight people on one group photograph who do not exist—and was kept only as an unreliable file superseded by the new identifications. It was replaced with 186 detected person and face boxes, side-by-side face comparisons against confirmed portraits, and Peter's review on 11 November confirming or naming 69 appearances across the album for Arvīds, Elvīra, Pēteris, Kathleen, Kalvis, Līga, Estella, Liga Irene and Peter. The photographs now appear on each person's page and in a new Photos gallery with a lightbox viewer and numbered face-box keys.
  • Correction (Arvīds's portrait). On 11 November 2026 Peter said that the photograph captioned "arvids danenbergs" in Raimonds Voronins's MyHeritage tree (MyHeritage tree) is not his grandfather Arvīds. That image was removed from Arvīds's page and recorded under not_this_person in his file (whether it shows his brother Žanis Danenbergs remains an open question for Pēteris Valentīns Danenbergs), and Arvīds's profile portrait is now a head-and-shoulders crop from the c. 1948 Christmas family photograph in the family album.
  • Site and archive infrastructure. After GitHub's 10-minute deploy limit kept timing out at ~600 MB, the live site danenberg.family moved from GitHub Pages to Cloudflare Pages, with GitHub Pages kept as a backup copy. Codified hermetic browser capture (make capture: MHTML, full-page screenshot and displayed text backed up to B2) after 409 URLs cited since 2 November had slipped through uncaptured, regenerating the capture plan across 1,110 cited URLs.
  • Archive requests and replies. Following NARA St. Louis's 10 November reply that Charles Hall's World War I claim file XC 198 015 is still in the custody of the Department of Veterans Affairs, a signed VA Form 20-10206 FOIA/Privacy Act request was mailed by certified mail on 13 November to the VA Evidence Intake Center in Janesville, Wisconsin. Replies received between 10 and 12 November were logged: the National Archives in St. Louis confirmed the claim file is still in VA custody, the VA's FOIA office pointed the request to the Veterans Benefits Administration, and the Puget Sound Regional Archives holds no vital records after 1907, so the 1916 Seattle birth image is likely out of reach.

  • Correction and redo (*Laiks* and Ohio newspapers). An initial 8 November search of the Latvian newspaper *Laiks*, other diaspora periodicals on Periodika.lv and Ohio local newspapers had worked from dirty OCR and small clips and invented details, dates, page numbers and quotes—reporting, for example, that Arvīds Danenbergs died on 22 February 1996 in a Kalamazoo hospital with a 2 March funeral at the Kalamazoo Latvian church and seven named eulogists who never spoke, that he was elected to the Latvian agronomists' audit committee in 1975, and that Elvīra eulogised an "Anna Šulce" in 1992 (the page actually reports the Grand Rapids funeral of Berta Buša, Laiks, 9 Dec 1992), while saving a photograph of General Oskars Dankers in Toronto as its clip of Elvīra's 1960 Kalamazoo sorority reunion. That sweep was quarantined and redone from 290 whole Periodika page images and whole Ohio newspaper PDFs, with a ledger auditing all 48 of its claims: 6 were confirmed as written (5 on the first pass and 1 once its Periodika issue ID was corrected), 40 were corrected from the whole pages, and 2 bare namesake mentions (a Viktors Danenbergs born 6 March 1925 in a 1958 tracing list and shipping employee Pauls Danenbergs in a 1955 account of the June 1941 deportations) were set aside as unlinked leads.
  • New records (Ohio and Michigan, Arvīds and Elvīra). Across 79 new sources read from whole pages, Seneca and Mercer County papers show Arvīds already serving as Seneca County Dairy Herd Improvement Association (DHIA) testing supervisor by 19 February 1951 (Republic Reporter, 22 Feb 1951), honoured at Columbus in March 1952 as the third-highest-ranking DHIA tester in Ohio—a Coldwater item linking the family's first sponsors on the Hawk and Smalley farms of Liberty Township to his Seneca County work (Mercer County Chronicle, 4 Apr 1952; Bellevue Gazette, 20 Mar 1952)—and honoured again at Ohio State University on 23 March 1961 as one of the two most outstanding DHIA production test supervisors (Republic Reporter, 23 Mar 1961). From 1956 to 1962 he also ran a sizeable Ohio dairy farm and herd in partnership with fellow Latvian agronomist A. Pladars (Republic Reporter, 26 Apr 1956, 30 Jul 1959, 23 Feb 1961; Bellevue Gazette, 31 Aug 1960; Laiks, 20 Aug 1958, 29 Aug 1962). In July 1958 the family lent their farm near Tiffin free of charge for a two-week summer camp of the Latvian correspondence school (Laiks, 26 Jul 1958), and in March 1960 the "Dannenbergs family in Tiffin" was named among the collectors who acquired works by Latvian painter Dainis Miezājs (Laiks, 12 Mar 1960). In Michigan, Arvīds lectured on dairy farming and farm economics at the Latvian Farmers' Days near Kalamazoo in 1960 (Laiks, 24 Sep 1960; Londonas Avīze, 7 Oct 1960; Latvija, 8 Oct 1960) and near Middleville in 1977 (Laiks, 17 Aug 1977; Latvija Amerikā, 10 Sep 1977), hosted the triennial meeting of the Latvian Agronomists' Society in the USA with Elvīra at their grain-corn farm near Marcellus on 9 August 1975 (Laiks, 23 Aug 1975), and served on the agronomists' audit commission in 1978, the same day Elvīra placed fourth in the rye-bread competition at Mežvidi (Laiks, 19 Aug 1978). They enrolled together in Decatur as American Latvian Association life members nos. 1654 and 1655 in 1978 (ALA Vēstis, Mar 1978; ALA Žurnāls, May 1978), funded a $300 music-study prize at the Garezers high school graduation in 1981 and 1982 (Laiks, 5 Sep 1981, 25 Aug 1982), and served across the 1980s and 1990s as officers of the Latvijas ciems (Garezers) Daugavas Vanagi chapter—Arvīds as chapter secretary and speaker on the Courland Pocket (1985) and Atis Kronvalds (1987), Elvīra as secretary and from 1984 chairwoman of the women's chapter (*vanadžu kopa*), hosting branch meetings and picnics at their farm and both receiving the Daugavas Vanagi Central Board's Certificate of Recognition (Laiks, 22 Sep 1982, 17 Mar 1984; Daugavas Vanagu Mēnešraksts, Jul 1985, Sep 1986, Nov 1987, Sep 1989, Sep 1991). Elvīra also appears in the 1960 Kalamazoo reunion photograph of the women's student association *Ausma* (Latvija Amerikā, 14 May 1960), in a 1987 jubilee notice recording her 1941 master's degree in pharmacy from the University of Latvia and her work as a pharmacist in Tiffin (Daugavas Vanagu Mēnešraksts, Jan 1987), and in later jubilee notices in Latvijas ciems (1997) and Milwaukee (2002, 2003, 2006; DVM, Jul 1997, Jan 2002, Mar 2003, Apr 2006). Days before his fatal car crash on 19 February 1996, Arvīds wrote a letter to *Laiks* pledging $1,000 as a pensioner to the fund supporting Latvia's National Guard (*Zemessardze*, Laiks, 2 Mar 1996), a gift remembered in his two obituaries alongside his death at Lee Memorial Hospital in Dowagiac and his 4 March memorial service at "Saulgrieži", Garezers (Laiks, 6 Apr 1996; Latvija Amerikā, 30 Mar 1996).
  • New records (Pēteris, Kalvis, Līga and the Sirlaks family). *Laiks*, *Latvija* and the fraternity journal *Universitas* carry Pēteris's graduation portraits and degree notices for his B.S. in chemistry at Case Institute of Technology in 1964 (Laiks, 15 Jul 1964; Universitas, Oct 1964), his M.S. at the University of Southern California in 1967 (Laiks, 23 Aug 1967), and his Ph.D. in bioorganic chemistry at the University of California, Santa Barbara followed by a cancer-research fellowship at the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 1970–1971 (Latvija, 26 Sep 1970; Universitas, Apr 1971), as well as youth athletics and camp self-government at the Grand Rapids camp "Daugava" in 1959–1960 (Laiks, 12 Sep 1959, 13 Aug 1960).
