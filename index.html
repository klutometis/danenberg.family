The Danenberg Family

This is the family history of Peter Danenberg's family: where they came from, how they crossed oceans, and how we know. It joins four lines: two from the farms of Vidzeme and Courland in Latvia, one from Ireland and from Texas, and one from the Pacific Northwest, where the American lines met. Every fact on a person's page carries its source, and every family link says how strong its evidence is.

Four family lines

Danenbergs

Peter's father's father's line, from Courland (Kurzeme) in western Latvia. Arvīds Danenbergs (1913–1996) was an agronomist. In 1944 he fled with his wife, their small son and his mother-in-law to Germany, lived four years in the Haunstetten displaced-persons camp at Augsburg, and in 1949 sailed from Bremerhaven to Boston. In Ohio he worked on a farm in Liberty Township, Mercer County, and then ran a dairy farm near Tiffin until its 1965 auction, before farming in Michigan. His parents were Alberts Danenbergs and Zelma Forstmane.

Sirlaks and Smukule

Peter's father's mother's line, from Rauna parish in Vidzeme. Elvīra Danenberga, née Sirlaka (1912–2011), a pharmacist, wrote the memoir of her life to 1949. Her father Mārtiņš Sirlaks (1883–1942) kept a shop in Cēsis; his family farmed Vīstuči at Jaunrauna for generations. Her mother was Emma Smukule (1886–1970).

Hall and McCabe

Peter's mother's father's line. Charles Henry Hall (1920–2001), a trucking maintenance manager who died in Orange County, California, was born in Vancouver, Washington, to Charles Hall, a Texan who probably served in the First World War, and Susan May McCabe (1886/1887–1972), born in Ireland, who came to America as a girl. Who their parents were is still an open question.

Estella, Alderson and Barnhart

Peter's mother's mother's line. Estella May Alderson (1916–2008) grew up in Portland as the daughter of Winter Scott Alderson and Carrie May Newman, whose own children, Lola and Elmer, were twenty years older, but she was adopted: her 1916 Washington birth certificate registers her in Seattle as Phillis May Barns. Her birth family is probably the Barnharts of Whatcom County, Washington, with Irene Mary Barnhart named as her probable mother.

Recent discoveries

  • Latvian citizenship restored. Pēteris Valentīns Danenbergs, born in Latvia in 1942, had his Latvian citizenship restored in 1993. Peter became a citizen in 2014 or 2015 and received his Latvian passport in September 2015. In 2021 Latvia's Office of Citizenship and Migration Affairs registered his children as citizens: Mneme, Kairos, Arete and Aletheia as descendants of citizens who left Latvia in 1944 fleeing the Soviet occupation, Chronos as a descendant of citizens of 17 June 1940, and Eos, born after her father was a citizen, confirmed in the register of citizens.
  • The crossing and first days in Ohio. Arvīds and Elvīra, their children Pēteris, Līga and Kalvis, and Elvīra's mother Emma Sirlaka sailed from Bremerhaven on the USAT General R. M. Blatchford on 28 June 1949 and landed at Boston on 7 July. A 14 July feature in the Fort Wayne Journal-Gazette shows them at the D. R. Smalley farm in Liberty Township, Mercer County, Ohio, sponsored by St. Paul's Lutheran Church: Arvīds trimming the lawn with his family, and farm manager Carl Hawk showing Elvīra and Emma an electric range.
  • Naturalization and the Tiffin dairy farm. After a year in Mercer County the family moved to Tiffin, Seneca County, Ohio. The Toledo Blade lists Arvīds and Elvīra among 84 people naturalized in US District Court in Toledo on 16 June 1955, the year Arvīds bought his farm near Tiffin. On 21 April 1965 ("Having sold the farm we will sell everything else"), he and co-owner Alfreds Pladars auctioned 75 registered Holsteins, dairy equipment and John Deere tractors before the move to Decatur, Michigan.
  • Edward Newman at Cold Harbor. On Estella's adoptive line, her grandfather Edward Newman (1844–1909) of Freethorpe, Norfolk, came to America between April 1861 and March 1863 (not in 1865, the year of his army discharge). He enlisted at Buffalo on 14 March 1863 in Company A, 8th New York Heavy Artillery, was severely wounded in the right elbow at Cold Harbor on 3 June 1864, and was discharged for wounds at Washington on 28 January 1865.
  • The Sirlaks farm and Rauna baptisms. Revision lists and church books carry the Sirlaks line at Jaunrauna back from Vīstuči to Mārcis Sirlaks, born about 1821, and his father Jānis, a manor weaver born about 1779, while Aina Sirlaka's 1998 memoir tells the farm's story. The German-language Rauna register records Emma Smukule (born 25 December 1885 Old Style, 6 January 1886 New Style, baptised 2/14 February 1886, daughter of farmhand Dahw Smukkul and Marie née Kahjon) and the children of Pēteris Sirlaks and Marie née Gibboy at Lejas Vīstuči.
  • Estella's 1916 birth certificate and the Barnharts. Washington birth certificate 146-1916-005906 settles that Estella May Alderson was born in Seattle on 5 May 1916 and registered as Phillis May Barns, daughter of "Irene Barns" (born in Washington, age 18) and "John Smith"; never amended for a court adoption, it sits beside the delayed certificate Estella herself filed in 1952, with affidavits from her adoptive mother and her sister Lola. The mother's Washington birthplace and a Walker–Arbuckle DNA cluster point to Irene Mary Barnhart (1899/1900–1971) of Lynden, Whatcom County, then 15 or 16.
  • Where the name came from, and a Baltic Y-DNA line. In the 7 June 1835 revision list for Gross-Bersen (Lielbērze) in Dobele district, Courland, the manor people appear "with their newly adopted family names", and family no. 6 is "Familie Dannenberg": the children of Jurre (Jurģis) Dannenberg (c. 1795–1832), who in 1826 had no surname and was "Jurre Tischler", Jurre the joiner. That this family is the origin of ours is likely but not yet proven: no record yet links it to Alberts Danenbergs, Arvīds's father. Peter's Y-DNA belongs to haplogroup N-Z16980, a Baltic line: the Danenbergs were Latvian manor serfs and craftsmen, not German immigrants.
  • Charles Hall, soldier. Draft records, a Veterans Administration card and 1917 California newspapers show a Charles Henry Hall, born in Comanche, Texas, in September 1890 or 1891, leaving El Portal, Mariposa County, for Camp Lewis in September 1917 and serving in the 316th Military Police Company before living in Vancouver, Washington. He is probably Peter's great-grandfather Charles Hall, and a DNA cluster on Peter's mother's side points to Comanche and Hamilton counties in central Texas.
