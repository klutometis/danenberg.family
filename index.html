The Danenberg Family

This is the family history of Peter Danenberg's family: where they came from, how they crossed oceans, and how we know. It joins four lines: two from the farms of Vidzeme and Courland in Latvia, one from Ireland and from Texas, and one from the Pacific Northwest, where the American lines met. Every fact on a person's page carries its source, and every family link says how strong its evidence is.

Four family lines

Danenbergs

Peter's father's father's line, from Courland (Kurzeme) in western Latvia. Arvīds Danenbergs (1913–1996) was an agronomist. In 1944 he fled with his wife, their small son and his mother-in-law to Germany, lived four years in the Haunstetten displaced-persons camp at Augsburg, and in 1949 sailed from Bremerhaven to Boston. In Ohio he worked on a farm in Liberty Township, Mercer County, and then ran a dairy farm near Tiffin until its 1965 auction, before farming in Michigan. His parents were Alberts Danenbergs and Zelma Forstmane.

Sirlaks and Smukule

Peter's father's mother's line, from Rauna parish in Vidzeme. Elvīra Danenberga, née Sirlaka (1912–2011), a pharmacist, wrote the memoir of her life to 1949. Her father Mārtiņš Sirlaks (1883–1942) kept a shop in Cēsis; his family farmed Vīstuči at Jaunrauna for generations. Her mother was Emma Smukule (1886–1970).

Hall and McCabe

Peter's mother's father's line. Charles Henry Hall (1920–2001), a trucking maintenance manager who died in Orange County, California, was born in Vancouver, Washington, to Charles Hall, a Texan who probably served in the First World War, and Susan May McCabe (1886/1887–1972), born in Ireland, who came to America as a girl. Who their parents were is still an open question.

Estella, Alderson and Barnhart

Peter's mother's mother's line. Estella May Alderson (1916–2008) grew up in Portland as the daughter of Winter Scott Alderson and Carrie May Newman, whose own children, Lola and Elmer, were twenty years older, but she was adopted: her 1916 Washington birth certificate registers her in Seattle as Phillis May Barns. Her birth family is probably the Barnharts of Whatcom County, Washington, with Irene Mary Barnhart named as her probable mother.

How we know

The site is built from 847 sources: Latvian church registers, estate revision lists and citizenship decisions, displaced-persons records from the Arolsen Archives, US censuses, draft cards, naturalization records and passenger lists, Irish civil registers, newspapers in Latvian and English, two family memoirs, and family trees. Recent discoveries

  • Marija Ģibola’s mother, Anna Mežciema, and three generations at Priekuļi. November 2026 On 19/31 October 1847 in Cēsis parish, Mārcis Ģibols married Anna Mežciema (“Bitter Anne Meschzeem”), confirming the maiden name of Marija Ģibola's mother. The 1811, 1826, and 1834 Priekuļi revisions trace her to Jānis Mežciems (c. 1786–1833) and Līze Mežciema, grandparents Jēkabs Mežciems (c. 1758–1832) and Maža Mežciema, and great-grandfather Jānis. The 1811 and 1816 lists show Mārcis Ģibols was the son of Pēteris Ģibols (c. 1765–1811/16) and grandson of Ansis Ģibols (c. 1742–1805).
  • Charles Hall, soldier. November 2026 The 19 April 1919 final payment roll links serial number 2274478 to a 22 September 1917 Mariposa induction, confirming that the El Portal draftee born in Comanche, Texas (12 September 1890/91) is Charles Henry Hall Sr.. Nine Army roster entries trace him at Camp Lewis in Company A, 316th Military Police and the 166th Depot Brigade, then from Armistice Day 1918 at Vancouver Barracks until 17 April 1919. Birth certificates place him and Susan May McCabe in Vancouver for Margaret Ellen (1919), Charles Henry (1920), and Catherine Elizabeth (1922).
  • Susan May McCabe’s 1972 death certificate and Peter McCabe. November 2026 Susan May McCabe's Oregon death certificate (died 6 February 1972 in Portland; informant Catherine) gives her birth as 9 April 1886 in Ireland and names her father Peter McCabe, confirming the family name and ruling out the Ringsend and Gortskeagh candidates. The Belturbet Peter McCabe married Catherine Prunty in 1869 and had daughters Susan (born c. 1885) and Margaret (died in Portland in 1935), but no son James Patrick. The link to our Susan is unproven; her Social Security application (SS-5, requested) should name her mother.
  • Anna Smukule, Elvīra’s godmother, and the Rauna and Jaunrauna records. November 2026 The Rauna birth register records Anna Smukule (born 3/15 September 1889 at Lapuļi to Dāvis Smukuls and Marija Kajone), confirming that Elvīra's 1912 godmother was Emma Smukule's younger sister. The registers also record Emma's 1885/86 birth at Lubāra, her parents' 1884 marriage, and all eight children of Pēteris Sirlaks and Marija Ģibola at Vīstuči. Jaunrauna revisions trace Mārcis Sirlaks (born c. 1821) and weaver Jānis Sirlaks (c. 1779–1830), alongside Aina Sirlaka's 1998 memoir.
  • Estella’s 1916 birth certificate and the Barnharts. November 2026 Birth certificate 146-1916-005906 shows Estella May Alderson was born in Seattle on 5 May 1916 as Phillis May Barns, daughter of "Irene Barns" (age 18, born Washington) and "John Smith", beside the 1952 delayed certificate Estella filed with Carrie May Newman and Lola. Mitochondrial DNA and a Walker–Arbuckle cluster point to Irene Mary Barnhart (1899/1900–1971) of Lynden (Estella's probable mother). Irene married Henry L. Meyer on 17 March 1917, raised two children, married Benjamin Larson in 1940, remarried Meyer in 1960, and died in Bellingham on 15 February 1971.
  • Where the Danenberg name came from, a Baltic Y-DNA line, and a Rūjiena family ruled out. November 2026 The 1835 Gross-Bersen revision in Courland lists "Familie Dannenberg", the children of Jurre Dannenberg (c. 1795–1832), who in 1826 was "Jurre Tischler", the joiner (likely surname origin). Peter's Y-DNA is haplogroup N-Z16980, confirming a Latvian rather than German paternal line. Meanwhile, Ede Kārkliņa née Danenberga was ruled out as kin to Alberts Danenbergs when Rūjiena birth and family registers placed her (born 19 April / 1 May 1860) in an unrelated Vidzeme family.
  • Latvian citizenship restored. October 2026 Pēteris Valentīns Danenbergs, born in Latvia in 1942, had his Latvian citizenship restored in 1993. Peter became a citizen in 2014 or 2015 and received his Latvian passport in September 2015. In 2021 Latvia's Office of Citizenship and Migration Affairs registered his children as citizens: Mneme, Kairos, Arete and Aletheia as descendants of citizens who left Latvia in 1944, Chronos as a descendant of citizens of 17 June 1940, and Eos, born after her father was a citizen, confirmed in the register.
  • The crossing, first days in Ohio, and the Tiffin dairy farm. October 2026 Arvīds, Elvīra, Pēteris, Līga, Kalvis, and Emma Sirlaka sailed from Bremerhaven on the USAT General R. M. Blatchford on 28 June 1949 and landed at Boston on 7 July. A 14 July feature in the Fort Wayne Journal-Gazette shows them at the Smalley farm in Liberty Township, Ohio, where Carl Hawk showed Elvīra and Emma an electric range. After moving in 1950/51 to Tiffin, Arvīds and Elvīra were naturalized on 16 June 1955, and on 21 April 1965 Arvīds auctioned 75 Holsteins before moving to Michigan.
